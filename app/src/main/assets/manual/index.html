1. Introduction

Soft Braille Keyboard replaces the on-screen keyboard with a six or eight dot braille keyboard. You type with your braille fingers on the touch screen exactly as you would on a Perkins brailler or the keyboard of a braille display. The keyboard speaks what you type and has gestures for moving around, reading, editing and selecting text.

It was created by Daniel Dalton, who is blind, for blind people. This version adds 64-bit support, the latest liblouis braille translation, TalkBack touch pass-through and an optional mode that uses the gestures of the TalkBack braille keyboard.

2. Setting it up

  1. Open the Soft Braille Keyboard app.
  2. Choose Enable Soft Braille Keyboard and turn it on in the system screen that opens, then go back.
  3. Choose Set Soft Braille Keyboard as the default keyboard and pick Soft Braille Keyboard.
  4. If you use TalkBack, also choose Enable touch pass-through for TalkBack. See the next section.

The keyboard now opens in any text field. The practice field in the app is a good place to learn.

3. Using the keyboard with TalkBack

With TalkBack on, explore by touch normally turns your touches into exploration and swallows multi-finger touches as TalkBack gestures, which makes braille typing impossible. On Android 11 and later the touch pass-through service fixes this: while the keyboard is expanded, touches on it go straight to the keyboard. As soon as the keyboard is shrunk or closed, TalkBack works normally again. The service does not read the screen or monitor what you do.

Turning on touch pass-through

  1. In the app choose Enable touch pass-through for TalkBack, or open Settings, Accessibility.
  2. Find Soft Braille Keyboard touch pass-through and turn it on.

Android 13 and later block accessibility services of apps that were not installed from an app store. If the switch is greyed out, open Settings, Apps, Soft Braille Keyboard, then the more options menu, and choose Allow restricted settings. Then try again.

Android only allows one pass-through area at a time. If another app that uses touch pass-through is active at the same time, turn it off if the keyboard stops receiving your touches.

Android 10 and earlier

Touch pass-through is not available. Suspend TalkBack while typing, for example with the TalkBack shortcut of holding both volume keys, and resume it when you are done. The keyboard speaks for itself, so you still hear everything you type.

4. Basic use

4.1 Keyboard layouts

There are two ways to hold the device.

  • Screen away (vertical layout). Best for phones. Hold the phone in landscape with the screen facing away from you. Your hands make two vertical lines on the screen: index fingers at the top, ring fingers at the bottom. Cradle the phone with your thumbs and little fingers. Your left index finger is dot 1 and your right index finger is dot 4, as usual.
  • Tabletop (horizontal layout). Best for tablets. Lay the device flat on a table and spread your six fingers from left to right across the screen, like on a braille keyboard.

The keyboard chooses a default layout from the screen size. You can change the dot arrangement in keyboard settings.

4.2 Calibrating the keyboard

To place the dots where your fingers naturally rest, put your fingers in position for the layout you want. Hold the three fingers of your left hand on the screen for about a second and release, then do the same with your right hand. You will hear which layout was set. Recalibrate at any time if your fingers trigger the wrong dots.

4.3 Typing

Type braille as you normally would. The keyboard picks a braille table for your language. You can choose another table and switch between computer braille and literary (contracted) braille in Braille Table Settings.

If the keyboard seems upside down, rotate the phone 180 degrees or turn on the Invert keyboard setting.

4.4 Shrinking the keyboard

You can shrink the keyboard to half the screen, for example to review suggestions from the app. The shrunk keyboard can't be typed on. With TalkBack, find it at the bottom of the screen and double tap to expand it again. Without TalkBack, tap it.

5. Classic swipe gestures

These are the original Soft Braille Keyboard gestures, used unless you choose the TalkBack gestures. Each dot can be swiped left, right, up or down. Some gestures hold one dot still while another finger swipes. Dots on the left (1, 2, 3) move and read by character, word and line; dots on the right (4, 5, 6) delete by the same amounts.

5.1 Moving around the text

5.3 Deleting

ActionGesture
Delete characterSwipe dot 4 left
Delete wordSwipe dot 5 left
Delete lineSwipe dot 6 left twice
Delete all textHold dot 3, swipe any right hand finger left, twice

Deleting a line or all text needs the gesture twice in quick succession, so you don't delete a lot by accident.

After typing, deleting removes the text before the cursor, like backspace. After moving or reading by character, word or line, it removes the text you just heard instead, and deleting again removes what comes after it.

6. Selecting text with the classic gestures

Move to one end of the text you want, then set the mark: hold dot 1 and swipe any right hand finger right. Move to the other end with the usual movement gestures. The text between the mark and the cursor is selected.

Swipe dot 6 right to hear the available actions and dot 6 up to perform the one you heard: speak selection, copy, cut, paste, delete or select all.

7. TalkBack braille keyboard gestures

If you already know the TalkBack braille keyboard, you can use its gestures here. In settings open Keyboard Settings, Gesture style and choose TalkBack braille keyboard. Typing and calibration stay the same.

These gestures count how many fingers swipe together, wherever they are on the screen. Directions are from your point of view: holding the phone screen away, right means towards your right hand. Gestures that hold a dot work with either hand: holding dot 1 is the same as dot 4, dot 2 the same as dot 5 and dot 3 the same as dot 6.

7.1 Basic gestures

ActionGesture
Add spaceSwipe right with one finger
Delete characterSwipe left with one finger
Add new lineSwipe right with two fingers
Delete wordSwipe left with two fingers
Move cursor back or forward by the reading granularitySwipe up or down with one finger
Submit text (send, search, go)Swipe up with two fingers
Hide keyboardSwipe down with two fingers
Keyboard menuSwipe up with three fingers
Switch to the next keyboardSwipe down with three fingers
Next or previous reading granularitySwipe right or left with three fingers

The reading granularities are characters, words, lines and spelling corrections.

A new line is always added as text, so it never sends a message, even in apps that send when you press enter. In a field that holds a single line, such as a search box, you hear "Single line field" instead. To send or search, swipe up with two fingers.

7.4 Correcting spelling

Swipe right or left with three fingers until you hear "Spelling corrections". Then:

Next or previous misspelled wordSwipe down or up with one finger
Next or previous suggestionSwipe right or left with one finger

7.5 The keyboard menu

Swipe up with three fingers to open a spoken menu with the features that have no TalkBack gesture: read all text, switch computer and literary braille, switch braille table, voice input, shrink the keyboard, word count, keyboard echo, keyboard feedback, auto-capitalisation, auto-correct, speak passwords, privacy screen, gesture practice, settings and this guide.

  • Swipe down or up with one finger to hear the next or previous item.
  • Swipe right with one finger to choose the item.
  • Swipe left with one finger to close the menu.

7.6 Mixed gestures

The mixed gesture style combines both sets. Choose Mixed in Gesture style.

  • Gestures that swipe with one finger, with or without holding a dot, are the classic gestures. For example swipe dot 4 left to delete, dot 1 right for the next character and dot 2 up to read the word.
  • Gestures that swipe with two or three fingers are the TalkBack gestures: new line, delete word, submit, hide the keyboard, the keyboard menu, switch keyboard, and all the selection and clipboard gestures, including those that hold a dot.

The two sets never overlap, because the classic gestures always swipe with a single finger. Reading granularity and correcting spelling with one finger are TalkBack features that need one finger swipes, so they are not available in this style; use the classic gestures of dots 1, 2 and 3 and the hold dot 4 spelling gestures instead. While the keyboard menu is open, it is navigated with one finger as described above.

7.7 Differences from the TalkBack braille keyboard

  • Calibrate with three fingers of each hand as described in calibrating the keyboard.
  • TalkBack opens a dialog for help and other actions. Here the menu is spoken inside the keyboard.
  • Custom gesture assignments of TalkBack are not copied; the default TalkBack gestures are used.

7.8 Practising gestures

Gesture practice lets you learn the gestures without changing your text. While it is on, every gesture says what it does and how it was recognised instead of doing it, for example "New line. swipe right with two fingers" or "Delete character. swipe dot 4 left". A gesture that does nothing tells you what the keyboard felt, for example "No action. swipe up with four fingers", which helps when a finger isn't being counted. Typing braille still works, so you can practise typing too.

  • Turn it on or off in the keyboard menu (swipe up with three fingers) with Turn gesture practice on or off. The menu still opens while practising.
  • Or use Gesture practice in Keyboard Settings. With the classic gestures, swiping dot 5 up twice still opens settings while practising.

When the keyboard opens with gesture practice on, it reminds you.

8. Settings

Open settings from the app's menu, from the keyboard (swipe dot 5 up twice, or the TalkBack keyboard menu) or from the system language and input settings.

8.1 Keyboard Settings

  • Default Keyboard Layout. Vertical (screen away), horizontal (tabletop) or automatic based on screen size.
  • Keyboard style. Normal; Slate and stylus mode, which swaps dots 1 2 3 with dots 4 5 6; or Swap dots 1 and 4 with dots 3 and 6.
  • Gesture style. Classic Soft Braille Keyboard gestures, the TalkBack braille keyboard gestures, or mixed.
  • Gesture practice. Gestures say what they do instead of doing it. See practising gestures.
  • Automatically select keyboard layout based on the position of your fingers. Picks the layout from where you place your fingers when calibrating.
  • Rotate keyboard with device. Lets the keyboard follow the screen rotation.
  • Invert keyboard. Turns the keyboard 180 degrees if it is upside down.
  • Show eight dot keyboard. Shows keys for dots 7 and 8 in computer braille, below dots 3 and 6.
  • Show circles on screen. Draws the keys on the screen. You don't need to hit them exactly.
  • Swipe sensitivity. How far you need to swipe for a gesture.

8.2 Braille Table Settings

  • Default Braille grade. Computer or literary braille.
  • Computer Braille table and Literary Braille table. The tables to type with. Literary tables include uncontracted and contracted braille.
  • Enable Braille tables for on the fly switching. The tables to cycle through with the switch table gesture or menu item.

8.3 Keyboard Feedback Settings

  • Keyboard Echo. Speak nothing, characters, words, or both as you type.
  • Keyboard Feedback. None, vibration, sound or both.
  • Sound theme. The sounds played when keyboard feedback includes sound. Minimal plays the same click for every action. Abstract plays a different tone for each kind of action: a tick for each character, a knock for space, a falling tone for delete, a rising chime for a new line, a swoosh for send, rising and falling notes for the menu, and a low tone for a pattern that isn't a character. Soft, Wood, Typewriter, Perkins, Bubble and Glass do the same in other styles, and the typing sound varies slightly from key to key. Themes you import are listed too.
  • Import a sound theme. Adds your own sounds as a theme and chooses it. Pick a zip of sound files, or several sound files, then name the theme. Name each file after the action it is for: type, space, delete, newline, submit, move, select, toggle, menu-open, menu-close and error, for example type.ogg or space.wav. OGG, WAV, MP3, M4A, AAC, FLAC and Opus files work. Numbered files such as type-1.ogg and type-2.ogg are played in turn at random. An action without a sound uses a similar one: send uses the new line sound, the menu and selection use the move sound, and space, delete, new line and move use the typing sound; errors use the Abstract error tone. A file named theme.properties can set name= and volume=, from 0 to 1. Other files are ignored.
  • Remove an imported sound theme. Deletes a theme you imported.
  • Speak passwords. When off, password characters are spoken as stars.
  • Echo misspellings as you type. Says "misspelled" after a misspelled word when you type a space.

8.4 Text To Speech Settings

The keyboard speaks on the accessibility volume, the same volume TalkBack uses, so it isn't affected by the media volume.

  • Speak through the screen reader. Off by default. When it is on and TalkBack, Jieshuo or another screen reader is running, the keyboard speaks with the screen reader's voice and settings instead of its own, so the settings below don't apply. Without a screen reader it speaks with its own voice.
  • Text To Speech Engine. Any engine installed on the device, or the system default.
  • Speech rate, Pitch and Volume. Adjust the voice of the keyboard without changing other apps. Volume is relative to the accessibility volume.
  • Test speech. Plays a sample with the current settings.
  • Engine settings. Opens the settings of the selected engine to choose voices, languages and download voice data.
  • System text-to-speech settings. Opens Android's text-to-speech settings.

8.5 Miscellaneous Settings

  • Auto-capitalisation. Capitalises the first word of each sentence.
  • Auto-correct. When you type a space after a misspelled word, it is replaced by the spell checker's best suggestion and you hear "corrected to" and the new word. To keep the word you typed, delete the space straight away: the word comes back and isn't corrected again while the keyboard is open. Passwords, addresses and fields that turn off suggestions aren't corrected. It needs a spell checker, chosen in the system language settings. It is off by default and can also be turned on or off in the keyboard menu.
  • Double-space period. Two quick spaces type a full stop and a space.
  • Privacy screen. Blacks out the screen while you type.
  • Voice input shortcut. Hold one finger still for two seconds to start dictation.
  • Offer another keyboard without a screen reader. When the keyboard opens while TalkBack or another screen reader is off, it asks whether to switch to another keyboard. This is useful when someone who can see uses the device. Choosing to keep the braille keyboard stops the question until a screen reader has been turned on again. It is off by default.
  • Vibrate when keyboard closes.

9. Supported braille tables

Braille translation uses liblouis 3.39.

Computer braille

Chinese, Chinese (Taiwan), Croatian, Danish, English (Canada), English (United States), Estonian, Finnish, French, German, Hebrew, Hungarian, Icelandic, Italian, Lithuanian, Norwegian, Polish, Portuguese, Portuguese (Brazil), Romanian, Russian, Slovenian, Spanish, Swedish and Turkish.

Literary braille

Arabic grade 1, Catalan grade 1, Czech grade 1, Danish grades 1 and 2, Dutch grade 1, English (UEB) grades 1 and 2, English (United States) grades 1 and 2, French (Canada) grades 1 and 2, French (France) grades 1 and 2, German (Germany) grades 0 and 1, German (Switzerland) grades 0 and 1, Greek grade 1, Hindi grade 1, Hungarian grade 1, Italian grade 1, Norwegian grades 0 to 3, Polish grade 1, Portuguese grade 1, Portuguese (Brazil) grade 1, Russian grade 1, Serbian grade 1, Slovak grade 1, Slovenian grade 1, Spanish grade 1, Swedish grade 1 and Vietnamese grade 1.

Serbian braille is the same for the Cyrillic and Latin alphabets, and is typed in Latin letters.

10. Troubleshooting

Patterns with three or more dots are not typed

Many phones have gestures using three fingers, such as swiping down with three fingers or touching and holding with three fingers to take a screenshot, or swiping up with three fingers for split screen. The phone takes every touch with three or more fingers for these gestures, so the keyboard never receives it and the pattern isn't typed. The keyboard tells you when this happens.

Turn these gestures off. Search the phone's settings for "screenshot" and "split screen" and turn off every gesture that uses three fingers. On Realme, Oppo and OnePlus phones they are usually under Additional settings, Screenshot.

TalkBack reads the screen instead of typing

Turn on touch pass-through. If typing is unreliable while another app that uses touch pass-through is active, turn that app's service off.
